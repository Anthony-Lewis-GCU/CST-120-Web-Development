Recovery

My journey toward recovery began in late 2017, when I finally admitted to myself that I had a problem with drug use. That moment of honesty was the first crack in the wall I had built around my pain. For the next five to six years, I wrestled with the belief that I could overcome addiction through sheer willpower — that I could be "strong enough" to quit on my own. But addiction doesn’t yield to pride or stubbornness. It deepened its grip on me, dragging me further into depression and desperation.

By late November 2022, I reached a breaking point. I knew I needed a change, so I made the difficult decision to leave my hometown and move down south to live with a friend. That move gave me a fresh start, and for six months, I maintained sobriety. It felt like a victory — until I let my guard down. I convinced myself I was "above the issue," and that false confidence led me back into addiction. It was then that I learned, painfully and personally, that addiction is a progressive disease. It doesn’t pause or rewind — it only moves forward, and so did my struggle.

In January 2024, I entered Touchstone Recovery Center in Fresno, California. Rehab was not a cure, but it was a lifeline. It gave me tools, perspective, and a community that understood the battle I was fighting. After completing treatment, I faced another challenge: finding a job that paid well enough to support me. It wasn’t easy, but I stayed committed to sobriety. I held onto the progress I had made, even when life tested me.

By September 2025, I had reached 19 months of sobriety — a milestone that marked not just time, but transformation. I moved back home to Sonoma County, carrying with me the strength I had earned through every setback and every triumph. Today, I no longer use cocaine. I’ve reclaimed my life, and I’m now pursuing a bachelor’s degree at Grand Canyon University in Arizona.

Recovery isn’t a straight line. It’s a winding path of self-discovery, humility, and courage. I’ve learned that asking for help is not weakness — it’s wisdom. And every day I choose sobriety, I choose a future that I once thought was out of reach.

Resource Hub

Inpatient Services:

Inpatient services for Substance Use Disorder (SUD) provide intensive, structured care in a residential setting for individuals struggling with addiction. These programs are designed for those who require 24/7 supervision, medical support, and a controlled environment to safely begin recovery.

    Here's a breakdown of what they typically include:
  • Medical Detoxification: Supervised withdrawal from substances with medical oversight to manage symptoms and ensure safety.
  • Therapeutic Support: Daily individual and group counseling, often using evidence-based approaches like Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and Motivational Interviewing.
  • Case Management: Coordination of care, including discharge planning and referrals to outpatient or community-based services.
  • Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT): For eligible individuals, medications like buprenorphine or methadone may be used to support recovery from opioid use disorder.
  • Peer Support and Recovery Coaching: Many programs integrate peer mentors or recovery coaches to provide lived-experience guidance.
  • Family Involvement: Education and therapy sessions for family members to support long-term recovery.
    Purpose and Goals:
  • Stabilize physical and mental health
  • Break the cycle of substance use in a safe, supportive environment
  • Build foundational coping skills and relapse prevention strategies
  • Prepare individuals for transition to outpatient care or sober living
Inpatient care is often recommended for individuals with severe addiction, co-occurring mental health disorders, or those who have not succeeded in outpatient settings.

Psychologists:

Psychologists play a vital role in supporting individuals with Substance Use Disorder (SUD), offering a range of services that address both the psychological roots of addiction and the skills needed for long-term recovery. Core Services Offered by Psychologists for SUD:

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Evaluating the severity of substance use, co-occurring mental health conditions, trauma history, and readiness for change.
  • Individual Therapy: Using evidence-based approaches like Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), and Motivational Interviewing to help patients understand triggers, manage cravings, and build coping strategies.
  • Group Therapy: Facilitating peer-based sessions that foster connection, accountability, and shared learning.
  • Family Therapy: Helping families understand addiction, improve communication, and rebuild trust.
  • Relapse Prevention Planning: Teaching patients how to recognize warning signs and develop strategies to maintain sobriety.
  • Psychoeducation: Providing information about addiction, recovery, and mental health to empower patients and reduce stigma.
  • Trauma-Informed Care: Addressing underlying trauma that may contribute to substance use through specialized therapies like EMDR or cognitive processing therapy.
  • Co-occurring Disorder Treatment: Managing depression, anxiety, PTSD, or other mental health issues alongside addiction.
The Psychologist’s Role in Recovery:
  • Understand the psychological drivers of addiction
  • Build emotional regulation and resilience
  • Reframe negative thought patterns
  • Strengthen motivation and self-efficacy
  • Navigate transitions between inpatient, outpatient, and community support

Phone Support Hotline:

24/7 recovery hotlines are lifelines for individuals facing substance use challenges, offering immediate, confidential support at any hour. These services are staffed by trained professionals or peer recovery specialists who understand the urgency and complexity of addiction.

Core Services of 24/7 Recovery Hotlines

  • Crisis Intervention: Immediate emotional support for individuals in distress, including those experiencing relapse, withdrawal, or suicidal thoughts.
  • Information & Education: Clear guidance on addiction, treatment options, and recovery pathways tailored to the caller’s situation.
  • Referral Services: Direct connections to detox centers, inpatient/outpatient programs, peer support groups, and mental health providers.
  • Motivational Support: Encouragement and validation to help callers take the next step toward recovery, especially during moments of doubt or fear.
  • Family Guidance: Support for loved ones seeking help on how to assist someone struggling with substance use.
  • Safety Planning: Help callers develop short-term plans to stay safe, avoid relapse, and access care.
Why They Matter:
  • Available 24/7, including holidays and weekends
  • Free and confidential
  • Often staffed by people with lived experience
  • Can be the first step toward treatment or long-term recovery

Peer Support Groups:

Peer support in the context of Substance Use Disorder (SUD) is a powerful, person-centered service where individuals with lived experience of addiction and recovery offer guidance, empathy, and hope to others on their journey. It’s not clinical—it’s relational, and often transformative.

What Peer Support Offers

  • Lived Experience Connection: Peer supporters have walked the path of recovery themselves, making their insights relatable and credible.
  • Emotional Support: They provide a safe space to share struggles, celebrate wins, and process setbacks without judgment.
  • Recovery Coaching: Helping individuals set goals, stay accountable, and navigate challenges using practical strategies.
  • System Navigation: Assisting with finding treatment programs, housing, employment, and other recovery resources.
  • Crisis Support: Offering calm, grounded presence during moments of relapse risk or emotional overwhelm.
  • Group Facilitation: Leading peer-led recovery meetings, workshops, or support circles.
  • Advocacy: Empowering individuals to speak up for their needs and rights within healthcare or legal systems.
Why It Matters:
  • Builds trust and reduces stigma
  • Fosters hope through shared stories
  • Complements clinical treatment with real-world wisdom
  • Often available in inpatient, outpatient, and community settings
